00 · My Perspective · 2026 Shah Alam → KL

Every morning starts staring at brake lights.

I am Muhammad Alif Najmi — a Business Analyst at axrail.ai commuting out of Selangor. Look straight ahead in the center of the screen: that wall of red tail lights is what car-centric planning forces millions of Malaysians to accept as normal.

Scroll slowly and watch what happens when we choose a different path — from trapped driver in 2026 to Malaysian public office by 2036.

01 · The Morning Reality RapidKL 750 · 18 km

18 km in 40 minutes. And elderly folks still can't cross safely.

In parts of Selangor, sidewalks crack and vanish. Elderly Malaysians and people in pain have to gamble with speeding lanes just to cross. Meanwhile, KKR still proposes new highways to "ease" KL congestion.

"Section 13, Shah Alam to Mid Valley is just 18 km — 40 minutes (2.22 min/km). You can add a bus every second, but if it shares congestion with cars, what's the point?"

02 · Pragmatic Urbanism Keep & Green

Does this mean destroying highways? No.

What has already been built stays. Watch the corridor in the center: instead of tearing down highways, we stop building new urban mega-highways — and wrap existing grey concrete blocks in greenery, rooftop gardens, and shaded walkways to cool the city and make it prettier.

06 · The 10-Year Horizon 2036 · Public Office

2036: in the seat where the nation's course actually changes.

After a decade of earning, studying, organizing, and building public trust, the goal is to stand in public office — making sure change doesn't stop at speeches, but reaches every street, bus stop, and train station ordinary Malaysians use every day.

PILLAR 01

Railways as the National Backbone

High-frequency LRT, MRT, reliable KTM Komuter, and intercity ETS rail connecting towns nationwide with seamless fare and walkway integration.

PILLAR 02

Enforced Bus Lanes Over New Highways

Halt urban mega-highway rubber-stamping. Physically enforce dedicated bus corridors like RapidKL 750 so buses beat private cars every morning.

PILLAR 03

Green Existing Concrete & Shaded Kaki Lima

Keep existing highways but wrap concrete pillars in tropical greenery, and build continuous tree-shaded sidewalks across Selangor and Malaysia.

PILLAR 04

Elderly-First Safe Crossings

Raised zebra crossings, generous pedestrian signal timings, and curb ramps so seniors, wheelchair users, and children cross without fear.

PILLAR 05

Protected Cycling & Shared Spaces

Physically separated bike lanes and human-scale public plazas so Malaysians can enjoy their neighborhoods every weekend.

PILLAR 06

Clean, Accountable Public Service

Zero tolerance for stealing public money. Open project tracking and leadership judged strictly by whether daily life improves on the street.

"In the end, when you die, you don't bring your wealth with you. People remember you for the good you built for others."
