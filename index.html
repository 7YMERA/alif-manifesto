00 · Public Record · Shah Alam & Kuala Lumpur · 2026

A country built for people, not just highways.

I am Muhammad Alif Najmi. Today in 2026, I am a Business Analyst commuting through Selangor's broken sidewalks. By 2036, I intend to stand in Malaysian public office — loud, unbought, and holding the pen to turn a car-centric nation into one where buses, bicycles, and pedestrians come first.

01 · The Daily Reality 2026 · Selangor & KL

Why do we keep building highways when people can't even cross the street?

Every single day I commute to work, I walk past the same ugly, cracked, disappearing sidewalks. In parts of Selangor, there are no proper walk paths and no safe street crossings at all. I love walking and I love cycling — yet our cities treat anyone outside a car like an afterthought.

What breaks my heart most isn't my own inconvenience. It is watching elderly Malaysians and people in pain forced to gamble with speeding traffic just to cross a few lanes of asphalt.

"Recently, KKR proposed even more highways to ease congestion in KL. Why pour billions into more concrete flyovers when enforced, beautified bus lanes move far more people?"

02 · Phase I · Year 1 2026–2027

Build financial independence and master real systems first.

Idealism without stability burns out fast — and a public servant who needs money from the wrong people can never serve the public honestly. 2026–2027 is about earning clean income, working hard, and learning how complex systems actually operate.

Fresh out of Universiti Teknologi PETRONAS (Computer Science Hons), I am currently working as a Business Analyst at axrail.ai in Kuala Lumpur — turning messy real-world processes into structured, working solutions.

  • 01
    Financial Base at axrail.ai Build savings, discipline, and professional credibility so future advocacy is never compromised by financial pressure.
  • 02
    Systems & Data Fluency Learn how organizations budget, specify, test, and deliver projects — skills Malaysian public administration desperately needs.
  • 03
    Grassroots Civic Tech (KakiLima) Keep shipping community tools like KakiLima to document unsafe sidewalks and connect Malaysians who care about walkable streets.
03 · Phase II · Year 3 2028–2029

Go deeper on knowledge. Join the NGOs. Fight loudly for buses.

By 2029, financial footing is in place — but my hunger for knowledge only grows. Complaining online isn't enough; I want rigorous, evidence-backed mastery of urban mobility, public policy, and transit economics.

Watch the 3D street behind this card transform as you scroll: dedicated emerald bus lanes are taking back space from gridlock, and shaded trees are returning to the walkway.

  • 01
    Pursue a Master's Degree Deepen formal expertise in public policy, urban transport systems, or data-driven governance to argue with ministry officials on equal footing.
  • 02
    Join Pedestrian & Transit NGOs Work directly with Malaysian walkability and public transport coalitions — auditing broken walkways in Selangor and pushing local councils (PBT) for fixes.
  • 03
    Become an Unmistakable Bus Advocate Champion buses as the fastest, most affordable, most equitable way to unclog KL and Selangor — demanding enforced bus lanes, real shelters, and reliable frequencies.
04 · Phase III · Year 5 2030–2031

Turn advocacy into undeniable public influence.

By 2031, the goal is to hold real leverage over the national conversation on how Malaysia moves. You don't strictly need to be a politician to shape policy — working inside a government office or ministry can move budgets too.

"Honestly, becoming a politician fits me better — because I want to be a loud complainer on behalf of ordinary people, and then actually hold the power to fix what's broken."
  • 01
    National Voice on Walkable Malaysia Build a trusted public platform exposing car-centric budgets, spotlighting elderly-hostile intersections, and showcasing real complete-street solutions.
  • 02
    Step Into Governance or Political Organizing Enter policy advisory, local council engagement, or political grassroots to learn legislation, municipal budgets, and constituency work from the inside.
  • 03
    Prove Complete Streets Work Push for pilot corridors with continuous shaded kaki lima, raised zebra crossings, protected cycling tracks, and strictly enforced bus lanes.
05 · The 10-Year Horizon 2036 · Public Office

2036: in the seat where the nation's course actually changes.

By 2036, after a decade of earning, studying, organizing, and building public trust, I aim to breach into public office — seasoned enough to deliver real execution, and bold enough to refuse "that's just how Malaysia is." Too many politicians speak beautifully about change while ordinary people on the street never feel it. That stops here.

PILLAR 01

Buses First, Highways Last

Halt urban mega-highway rubber-stamping. Redirect funds into physically enforced, beautified bus rapid transit lanes and reliable first-mile feeder buses.

PILLAR 02

Continuous Shaded Kaki Lima

Mandate connected, obstacle-free, tree-shaded sidewalks across Selangor and every Malaysian town — maintained like essential infrastructure, not afterthoughts.

PILLAR 03

Elderly-First Safe Crossings

Build raised zebra crossings, generous pedestrian signal timings, and curb ramps so seniors, wheelchair users, and children never have to sprint across traffic.

PILLAR 04

Protected Cycling & Shared Spaces

Reclaim asphalt for physically separated bike corridors and vibrant public plazas where communities actually enjoy being outside.

PILLAR 05

Clean, Accountable Public Service

Zero tolerance for stealing public money. Open project tracking, loud public accountability when contractors cut corners, and leadership judged strictly by whether daily life improves on the street.

"In the end, when you die, you don't bring your wealth with you. People remember you for the good you built for others."
